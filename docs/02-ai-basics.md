# 1. AI basics in plain English

**What is AI, really?** Think of it as a very advanced autocomplete. You type
something, and the AI guesses what comes next — one word at a time — based on
patterns it learned from a huge amount of text. It's not thinking, it's not
conscious, and it doesn't know you. It's just very good at sounding helpful.

## The tools you'll hear about

| Term | What it means in plain English |
|------|-------------------------------|
| AI / artificial intelligence | Software that generates text, images, or code by pattern matching. |
| LLM / large language model | The engine behind chatbots. Reads and writes text. |
| Prompt | What you type to the AI. The better your prompt, the better the answer. |
| Chatbot | A program you talk to in a conversation window. |
| Local AI | AI that runs on your own computer instead of a company's server. |
| Agent | An AI that can do a small task for you, like sending a draft email. |
| MCP | A way for AI tools to connect to your files and apps safely. |

## Where to start

You don't need to install anything. Open a free chatbot (for example, the one at
chat.openai.com or claude.ai) and try this:

> "I work unpredictable shifts as a delivery driver. Help me make a simple
> weekly plan that fits around changing hours."

Read the answer. Ask follow-ups. That's it — you're using AI.

## What AI is good at (and bad at)

**Good at:** drafting messages, summarizing long text, brainstorming, formatting
messy notes, explaining things step by step.

**Bad at:** exact math unless you double-check, remembering private facts unless
you tell it, giving legal or medical advice, knowing your schedule unless you
share it.

**Golden rule:** never paste your Social Security number, bank account number,
passwords, or patients' private information into a public chatbot. See
[local AI and privacy](./06-local-ai-privacy.md) for safer options.
