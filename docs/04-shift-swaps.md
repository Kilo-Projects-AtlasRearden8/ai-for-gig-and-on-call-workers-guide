# 3. Shift swaps

Irregular schedules mean you sometimes need to trade a shift — you can't make
Thursday, but a coworker can. AI can help you write the message, track the swap,
and protect your pay.

## Before you swap

Check your employer's or platform's rules first. Some jobs require manager
approval; some apps don't allow swaps at all. A quick message to your scheduler
can save a headache later.

## What to include in a swap request

A good swap message says:

- Your name
- The shift you want to give away (date, start, end)
- The shift you want to pick up (if any)
- Why you're asking (keep it brief — "family commitment" is enough)
- What you're offering in return (if anything)
- How to reach you

## AI draft example

Paste this into a chatbot, filling in your details:

> "Write a short, polite shift-swap request to a coworker. I work as a nurse on
> call. I need to swap a Thursday 7pm–7am shift for a Friday 7am–7pm shift. I'm
> happy to pick up one of their future shifts in return. Keep it under four
> sentences, friendly, and professional. Don't include my real name — use
	[Your Name]."

The AI gives you a draft. Read it, adjust it, send it. Never send anything you
haven't read yourself.

## Track your swaps

Write down every swap:

- Date of the swap
- Who you swapped with
- Original shift and new shift
- Whether a manager approved it
- Date confirmed

Use `worksheets/shift-swap-log.html` or a notes app. If pay ends up wrong, your
log is your proof.

## A warning about pay

When you swap shifts, make sure your pay rate moves with the shift. A Friday
day shift might pay differently than a Thursday night shift. Confirm the rate
before you agree, and check your next paystub.
